## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is trying to help. Say what you mean without
being unpleasant about it.

That is the whole thing. The rest of this page exists so that "be decent" has a
definition when somebody argues about it.

## What is expected

**Talk about the work, not the person.** "This will break on a slow connection"
is useful. "Did you even test this" is not, and it is the same sentence with the
help removed.

**Assume competence.** Someone filing a confusing bug report is usually
confused, not stupid. Someone whose patch you disagree with usually had a reason
you have not heard yet.

**Take a no.** Not every idea belongs in the project, and not every patch gets
merged. A maintainer declining something is not an insult and not an invitation
to argue it in three more threads.

**Let people be wrong quietly.** Correct the thing, not the record. Nobody needs
a tally of their past mistakes.

**English is not everyone's first language.** Read for what someone meant.
Sharpness that comes from a limited vocabulary is not rudeness.

## What is not

- personal attacks, insults, or contempt dressed up as directness
- harassment of any kind, in the open or in private messages
- sexualised language or imagery, anywhere
- prejudice about who someone is - nationality, race, sex, gender, sexuality,
  disability, religion, age, or how they look
- publishing anyone's private information
- following someone from thread to thread to keep an argument going
- deliberately wasting people's time

"It was a joke" does not change how it landed.

## Where this applies

Anywhere the project happens: issues, pull requests, discussions, commit
messages, code review, and the Discord server if there is one. It also applies
when you are representing the project somewhere else.

It does not extend to your life. What you say elsewhere is your business, right
up until it is aimed at someone here.

## Reporting

> [!NOTE]
> Something that is a conduct problem *and* a security problem should go through
> [the private advisory route](https://github.com/Rathio12/LuwanTerm/security/advisories/new)
> instead, so it is not public while it is being dealt with.

If something needs attention, the maintainer is
[@Rathio12](https://github.com/Rathio12). Reports can be made by opening a
private security advisory - it is the one channel here that is not public - or
by any means you already have.

Say what happened and where. You do not have to prove anything, argue a case, or
have been the target of it. Reporting something on someone else's behalf is
fine.

**Reports are handled privately.** Nobody is named publicly, and there is no
requirement to make a statement.

## What happens then

This is a small project run by one person, so there is no committee and no
appeals board. What there is:

1. **A word.** Most things are a misunderstanding or a bad day, and end here.
2. **A warning.** Said plainly, with what needs to change.
3. **A block.** From a thread, or from the project.

Serious things - harassment, threats, publishing someone's private information -
skip straight to the last one.

## A note on the maintainer

This applies to whoever runs the project as much as to anyone contributing. If
the maintainer is the problem, saying so is legitimate, and "it is my project"
is not an answer to it.
